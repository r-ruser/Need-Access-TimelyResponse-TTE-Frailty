# Methods/results summary v6

The analysis separates a policy-process layer from a target-trial layer. The policy-process layer estimates Need-standardized probabilities of an adequate Early response across baseline Access contexts using marginal standardization and person-level bootstrap uncertainty. The target-trial layer compares Early, Delayed and Persistent-unmet response strategies using stabilized treatment weights, outcome-observation weights, 1st/99th percentile truncation, survey weighting and person-clustered robust inference. Risk differences use survey-weighted linear-probability models and risk ratios use modified-Poisson models.

The HRS 2 × 2 decomposition produced Early-versus-Persistent risk differences of +0.66 percentage points for PA+SMK+ALC/H-mPFI-28, +0.25 for PA+SMK/H-mPFI-28, −3.77 for PA+SMK+ALC/common-FI20 and −3.09 for PA+SMK/common-FI20. Need-definition shifts were small, whereas outcome-definition shifts were −4.43 and −3.34 percentage points. All four analyses passed support, balance and positivity gates.

HRS Need-standardized Early-response probability was 3.63 percentage points higher for higher versus lower wealth (95% bootstrap interval 0.82 to 6.23). Common-construct estimates pointed in the same direction in HRS (−3.09, 95% CI −9.03 to 2.85) and CLHLS (−2.45, −15.25 to 10.36). The cohort comparison is descriptive heterogeneity, not a causal health-system effect.

Increasing modeled Early coverage to 50%, 70% and 90% generated small and highly uncertain frailty-or-death differences of +2.1, +3.6 and +5.2 events per 1,000 relative to observed coverage. These are observational policy-impact scenarios, not policy implementation effects.
